Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions we hear most about the FPU.

Is an FPU a GPU?

No. A GPU executes programs over large batches of data with heavy synchronization. An FPU never batches: tokens flow through a statically placed pipeline and results emerge continuously. The FPU is faster when your workload is a stable pipeline; the GPU is faster when it is a large one-shot computation.

Can the FPU do general-purpose computing?

It does not replace a CPU. FlowLang has no recursion, no dynamic memory allocation, and no unbounded loops — by design. Control flow is limited to bounded conditionals inside kernels. Anything irregular belongs on the host.

Does FPU support floating point?

Yes. Standard kernels include IEEE 754 single- and double-precision math, plus bf16 and fixed-point formats typical for signal processing and ML feature work.

What happens to in-flight tokens when I stop a pipeline?

pipeline.stop() drains gracefully: sources stop emitting, fabric queues empty, and sinks flush before the bitstream is unloaded. Nothing in flight is lost. To abort immediately, use pipeline.abort(), which resets all queues.

How do I run multiple pipelines on one device?

The placer partitions cores and fabric channels between pipelines, and each gets an isolated flow namespace. All pipelines share HBM capacity, so budget your window and scratchpad usage across them. You can also chain devices over FlowLink for larger topologies.

Is the simulator accurate?

fpu-sim is cycle-accurate for fabric, queues, and standard kernels; timing for custom GDK-C kernels is within 5% of measured silicon. Throughput margins caused by host I/O differ, so re-benchmark on hardware before capacity planning.

What does the "FPU" name stand for here?

Flow Processing Unit. The historical meaning — floating-point unit — was a nice coincidence: our first silicon also did a lot of floating point, just as part of the flow.
